\subsection{Reading the bitmasks}

Each factor is a $4\times4$ matrix over $\FF$ encoded as an integer: bit $4i+j$ (least significant bit
first) holds entry $(i,j)$. For example, $a_t = 1$ is the matrix with a single 1 in position $(0,0)$, and
$a_t = 4096$ is the matrix with a single 1 in position $(3,0)$. Term $t$ contributes the rank-one tensor
$a_t \otimes b_t \otimes c_t$ under the index convention of Equation~(1) of the main paper, with the $a$
factor indexed $(i,j)$, the $b$ factor $(j,k)$ and the $c$ factor $(k,i)$.

\section{Encoding details}

\subsection{Instance construction}

A census or rewrite instance is fixed by a triple: a group $G \leq \GL(4,2)^3 \rtimes S_3$, a multiset of
subgroups $H_1,\dots,H_m \leq G$ (the \emph{slot list}) with $\sum_i [G:H_i]$ equal to the target rank,
and, for a rewrite instance, a residual tensor obtained by subtracting the kept orbits from the target.
The encoder emits:

\begin{itemize}
\item $3n^2$ Boolean variables per slot for the representative term's factor bits, constrained so that
the representative is fixed by its slot's subgroup;
\item Tseitin variables for each distinct degree-3 monomial $a[i,j]\,b[j,k]\,c[k,i]$ appearing anywhere,
defined by the usual AND clauses;
\item one XOR constraint per $G$-orbit of tensor coordinates, expanded into CNF, equating the sum of
monomials over all terms with the residual value at a representative coordinate;
\item nonzero clauses forbidding an all-zero factor on any axis of any slot, which is sound because a
term with a zero factor contributes nothing and can be deleted, lowering the rank.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The two exact reductions}

\textbf{E1 (orbit equations).} An encoded scheme is a sum of complete $G$-orbits of terms, so the value
of the encoded tensor at coordinates $P$ and $gP$ is the same expression for every $g \in G$. Emitting
one equation per orbit of coordinates is therefore an exact rewriting, not a relaxation. For $\Gamma$ at
$n=4$ this replaces the 4096 equations by 379.

\textbf{E2 (gate sharing).} The same degree-3 monomials recur across the surviving equations. Introducing
one Tseitin variable per distinct monomial, cached across the whole instance, removes the duplication.

\textbf{Validation.} Both reductions were validated by (i) recovering AlphaTensor's 47 from its own orbit
type under the reduced encoding, with the decoded scheme identical to the input and host-verified, and
(ii) an exhaustive $n=2$ cross-check against the unreduced encoder: 44 of 44 census instances agreed on
verdict. Measured effect on a from-scratch $4	imes4$ census instance of an order-12 group with orbit
type $[12,12,12,6,3,1]$:

\subsection{Blocking solutions}

Same-rank searches must exclude the input scheme, and enumeration of distinct schemes must exclude what
has already been found. Blocking the satisfying \emph{assignment} is insufficient: the same term set
reappears under a different assignment of representatives to slots. We block at the \emph{set} level,
adding for each blocked scheme a clause asserting that at least one term differs, using equality gadgets
over the decoded term bits. An earlier version of this campaign blocked at the assignment level, which
caused two over-counts that were found and corrected before publication: a set of same-rank $4\times4$
instances that had returned only relabelings had not in fact been decided (closed afterwards by 237
UNSAT instances), and a set of 15 result files at $5\times5$ was found to contain 6 distinct schemes
rather than 15.

\section{The rank-49 attractor}

All 3{,}257 host-verified rank-49 schemes found by three different flip dynamics have the factor-rank
signature $\{1{:}36,\,2{:}12,\,4{:}1\}$ on each of the three axes --- identical to
Strassen$^{\otimes2}$'s. Since the signature is a $\GL(4,2)^3$-invariant, they all lie in the same
equivalence family, and no walk left it.

Two steering strategies were tested and retired:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Forbid rank-4 factors.} The gated walk did leave the attractor (floor 50, 2{,}278 distinct
signatures), but 5{,}883 of 6{,}000 rank-50 ancestors of the 47 --- generated by plus transitions from the
47 itself --- contain a rank-4 factor, and the gated pool shares zero signatures with the ancestor set.
The gate excludes the target's basin by construction.
\item \textbf{Stay in the $\tau$-quotient.} From a trivial start the quotient walk plateaus at rank 55
with 13 $\tau$-fixed terms and 21 pairs, whereas every symmetric rank-55 ancestor of the 47 has 11 fixed
terms; the two sets of 1{,}594 and 1{,}371 signatures share nothing.
\end{itemize}

A useful by-product of the second attempt: in the $\tau$-quotient, without a move that changes a fixed
term's $a$-factor, the fixed-term count is frozen and only even ranks are reachable. The identity
$(a+e)(a+e)^{\mathsf T} = aa^{\mathsf T} + ae^{\mathsf T} + ea^{\mathsf T} + ee^{\mathsf T}$ yields four
symmetric moves, two of which are $3\to2$ reductions the plain flip graph cannot express. They lifted
$\tau$-symmetric $3\times3$ walks from 25 to 23 and freed the parity at $4\times4$, changing the floor
from 56 to 55.

\section{Process findings}

These are engineering observations, recorded because they cost real time to learn and are not specific
to this problem.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.42\textwidth}p{0.52\textwidth}}
\toprule
Finding & Rule adopted \\
\midrule
Conflict budgets do not bound wall time: instances with a $10^6$-conflict budget ran 15--53 minutes when
the conflict rate collapsed. & A wall-time watchdog per instance, independent of the conflict budget. \\
Decidable instances decide fast; budgets are consumed by undecidable ones. & Short sweep budgets, then
targeted re-runs at $10^{8}$ conflicts only for recorded exhaustions. \\
Conjugacy deduplication of stabilizers is exact and large: 9{,}605 $\to$ 1{,}763 instances in one sweep,
87{,}371 $\to$ 5{,}737 in another. & Always enumerate over conjugacy classes, never over subgroups. \\
Folding kept structure into a residual: 574{,}000 $\to$ 4{,}400 variables. & Never encode what is
already decided. \\
Assumption-based incremental SAT was slower than fresh instances (822\,s versus 3.5\,s on the same
instance). & Fresh instances, with the encoding template cached. \\
Lex-leader symmetry breaking is sound but direction-dependent: about $2\times$ faster on UNSAT,
$10$--$64\times$ slower on satisfiable controls. & Opt-in, for refutation sweeps only. \\
12 physical cores, not 24: 24 processes cut throughput about sixfold. & At most 12--16 solver workers on
small instances, 6--8 on large ones. \\
Static striding creates stragglers: 2 of 12 workers held 249 instances. & A cost-sorted work queue with
a per-instance wall cap. \\
Two PySAT bindings misbehaved: Kissat 4.0.4 returned a wrong answer on a five-clause formula and then
crashed; CaDiCaL 3.0.0 crashed silently. & Both excluded; all results use CaDiCaL 1.9.5. \\
